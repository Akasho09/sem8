# N-gram Language Models

## 🔹 1. Why Do We Need Language Models?
- 👉 We needed a way to compute:
- Probability of a sequence of words
- 📌 Motivation Example
- Sentence:
- "The office is about fifteen minuets from my house"
- “minuets” = valid word
- ❌ But incorrect here
- 👉 Need context-based probability to choose:
- ✔ minutes
- ❌ minuets

## 🔹 2. What is a Language Model?
- A Language Model (LM) computes:
    - P(w1, w2, w3, ..., wn)
- 👉 Probability of a sentence
- 🔹 Also Used For Prediction
    P(wn | w1, w2, ..., wn-1)
- 👉 Predict next word

## 🔹 3. Applications
1. Speech Recognition
“I saw a van” vs “eyes awe of an”

2. Machine Translation
“high winds” vs “large winds”

3. Autocomplete / Predictive Text
“turn off your cell ___” → phone

4. Spelling Correction (context-based)

## 4. Chain Rule of Probability
- We expand sentence probability:
P(w1, w2, ..., wn)
as:
- P(w1) × P(w2|w1) × P(w3|w1,w2) × ... × P(wn|w1,...,wn-1)
- ⚠️ Problem
Too many dependencies
Rare sequences → unreliable

## 🔹 5. Markov Assumption (Simplification)
👉 Only depend on last k words
P(wi | w1...wi-1) ≈ P(wi | wi-k...wi-1)

## 🔹 6. N-Gram Models
- Use (n-1) previous words
- 🔹 Types
| Model   | Context    |
| ------- | ---------- |
| Unigram | No context |
| Bigram  | 1 word     |
| Trigram | 2 words    |
- 🔹 Relation
👉 N-gram = (n−1) order Markov model

## 🔹 7. Example Simplification
- Instead of:
- P(office | about fifteen minutes from)
- Use:
- P(office | from)   → Bigram
- OR:
- P(office | minutes from) → Trigram

## 8. Limitation of N-Grams
- 👉 Cannot capture long-distance dependencies
- Example:
"The computer ... crashed"
“crashed” depends on “computer”
Not captured by small n-grams

## 🔹 9. Estimating Probabilities (MLE)
📌 Bigram Formula
P(wi | wi-1) = Count(wi-1, wi) / Count(wi-1)

## 🔹 10. Example
Corpus:
"I am here"
"I am happy"
"I would like to know"
- 🔹 Calculation
P(I | <start>) = 2/3

## 🔹 11. Sentence Probability
Sentence:
"I want English food"
🔹 Using Bigram
P(I|<start>) × P(want|I) × P(English|want) × P(food|English)

## 🔹 12. What N-Grams Capture
Grammar patterns
Word combinations
Domain knowledge
- 📌 Examples
“I want” → high probability
“Chinese food” > “English food”

## 🔹 13. Practical Issues
1. Underflow Problem
Multiplying small values:
0.0001 × 0.0002 × ...
👉 Result ≈ 0
- ✅ Solution: Log Space
log(P1 × P2) = log(P1) + log(P2)

2. Zero Probability Problem
If any bigram unseen:
P = 0
👉 Entire sentence probability becomes 0 ❌
- 🔹 Solution
👉 Use Smoothing (next topic)
