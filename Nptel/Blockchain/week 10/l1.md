# Bridges in Blockchain
- A blockchain bridge is a protocol that connects two separate blockchain networks and enables:
    - transfer of cryptocurrencies/tokens,
    - sharing of data,
    - execution of cross-chain operations,
    - `interoperability` between blockchains.
- Bridges allow assets and information to move between otherwise isolated blockchain ecosystems.
- Example:
- Moving assets from Ethereum to Binance Smart Chain.

## Blockchain Interoperability
- Blockchain interoperability is the ability of different blockchain networks to:
    communicate with each other,
    exchange data,
    transfer assets/tokens,
    and execute cross-chain operations seamlessly.
- It allows independent blockchains like Bitcoin, Ethereum, and Polkadot to interact without relying on a centralized intermediary.

## 3. Asset
- Anything of value stored in blockchain.
- Examples:
    Bitcoin
    Ether
    Tokens
    Digital ownership

## 4. Asset Transfer
- Moving asset value from:
    one blockchain to
    another blockchain
- Example:
    - 1 BTC → equivalent ETH

## 5. Cross-Chain Transfer
- Transfer of assets/data across two different blockchain networks.
- Example:
    - Bitcoin network ↔ Ethereum network

## 6. Asset Exchange
- Two parties exchange assets across different blockchains.
- Example:
- Alice sends 1 BTC
    Bob sends 33 ETH
- Both transfers must succeed together.

## 7. Interoperability in Permissionless Blockchains
- Main concern:
    cryptocurrency/asset transfer
- Example:
    - BTC converted into ETH

## 8. Interoperability in Permissioned Blockchains
- Main concern:
    - transferring verified consensus-backed data
- Important:
    - Destination network must trust that:
- transferred data already achieved consensus in source network.

## 9. Consensus-Driven Data Transfer
- Transferred data should represent:
    agreed network state
    validated ledger information
- Not:
unconfirmed/private data

## 10. Isolated Silos
- Different blockchains work independently.
- Problems:
    no direct communication
    incompatible standards
    protocol differences

## 11. Protocol Translation
- Different blockchains use:
    different consensus
    different transaction formats
    different rules
- Interoperability requires:
    translation/adaptation mechanisms

## 12. Autonomy of Blockchains
- Each blockchain:
    keeps its own protocol
    keeps its own governance
- Example:
    Bitcoin cannot force Ethereum to change protocol.

## 13. Trusted Third Party (TTP)
- External trusted intermediary that facilitates transfer.
- Example:
    centralized exchange
    bank
